Infinite-slope reliability · Monte Carlo

Landfill slope stability under a rising leachate head

Ten thousand synthetic slopes per scenario. Cohesion, friction angle and unit weight are drawn once and shared across all three scenarios, so the only thing separating them is how much leachate sits above the liner.

Inputs

Cross-section

The slab of waste above the liner, drawn to the current geometry. Each coloured line is one scenario's mean leachate head.

Factor of safety

Distribution across all samples, 50 shared bins. Anything left of the dashed line has slid.

Sensitivity to leachate head

Failure probability as the mean head sweeps from a dry liner to a fully saturated slab, holding the baseline scenario's coefficient of variation. Markers sit at each scenario's own result, which uses its own spread — so they land near the curve, not exactly on it.

Site rainfall

Daily rainfall at the site, fitted as a lognormal from the sample mean and standard deviation alone, then accumulated over the antecedent window to drive the leachate head. The fitted median is a genuine check: it was never used in the fit, so it either lands on the sample median or the family is wrong. Days are drawn independently, which no real rainfall record is — storms cluster — so treat the spread of the window total as a lower bound. If the sample counted only rain days, drop the wet-day fraction on the left to match.

Observed rainfall through the model

The measured antecedent rainfall of each real failure, converted to a head rise by the two coefficients on the left, then run through this slope's failure curve. The head is taken as rainfall alone on a dry liner, so the last column is what that rain adds by itself. It answers a narrow question — given only how much rain fell, what would this model have said? — so a low number next to a real disaster is a statement about the model, not about the site.

Summary

Reliability index β is (mean FS − 1) ÷ std dev FS — how many standard deviations of margin sit between the slope and failure.

Scenario Mean head Mean FS Median FS Std dev FS 5th pct FS Reliability β Failures P(failure)

Method

σ = γ·z·cos²β total normal stress on the slip plane u = γ_w·H·cos²β pore pressure from the leachate head σ′ = max(σ − u, 0) effective normal stress τ_r = c′ + σ′·tan φ′ shear resistance τ_d = γ·z·sin β·cos β driving shear stress FS = τ_r / τ_d · failure when FS ≤ 1

Cohesion and leachate head are lognormal, so they stay positive and skew right. Friction angle and unit weight are normal, clipped to 1–50° and to a floor of 1 kN/m³ respectively. Head is capped at the slab depth — the leachate cannot rise above the waste.

The random number stream is seeded, so a given seed and sample count reproduce exactly. Material properties are drawn once and reused by all three scenarios, which is what makes the comparison a controlled one: the same ten thousand slopes, wetter or drier.
