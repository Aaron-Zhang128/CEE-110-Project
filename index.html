Landfill slope stability under transient rainfall infiltration

Rainfall is pushed into the waste with the TRIGRS v2.0 saturated model — Iverson's linearised solution for the pressure head that arrives at the slip surface — and the resulting head is carried into the infinite-slope factor of safety. Cohesion, friction angle and unit weight are drawn once and shared across all three scenarios, so the only thing separating them is the rain and the drainage.

Sensitivity to rainfall

Failure probability as the storm intensity sweeps from nothing upward, one curve per scenario water table. The gap between the curves is what drainage buys you: the same rain on a slope drained two metres deeper is a different risk. Each curve flattens once ψ hits its ceiling at the ground surface.

Site rainfall

Daily rainfall at the site, fitted as a lognormal from the sample mean and standard deviation alone, then run day by day through the same TRIGRS response that drives the scenarios. The fitted median is a genuine check: it was never used in the fit, so it either lands on the sample median or the family is wrong. Days are drawn independently, which no real rainfall record is — storms cluster — so treat the spread of the window total as a lower bound. If the sample counted only rain days, drop the wet-day fraction on the left to match.

Method

D_1 = D_0 / cos²δ vertical diffusivity β = cos²δ − I_ZLT/K_S steady-state gradient Rf(τ) = 2√(D_1·τ/π)·exp(−Z²/4D_1τ) − Z·erfc[ Z / 2√(D_1·τ) ] ψ(Z,t) = (Z − d)·β + Σ (I_n/K_S)·[ Rf(t−t_n) − Rf(t−t_n₊₁) ] FS(Z,t) = tan φ′/tan δ + [ c′ − ψ(Z,t)·γ_w·tan φ′ ] / [ γ_s·Z·sin δ·cos δ ] failure when FS ≤ 1

This is TRIGRS v2.0 (Baum, Savage & Godt, USGS Open-File Report 2008-1159) in its saturated form: Iverson's (2000) linearised solution to Richards' equation, with each day of rain entering as its own rainfall period and the periods superposed. Z and d are vertical depths; surface flux is capped at K_S and the excess runs off; ψ is capped at Z·cos²δ, which is the water table standing at the ground surface. Above the water table ψ is negative — the saturated model reports that suction rather than solving the unsaturated zone, which is TRIGRS's own caveat for this option.

The factor of safety is TRIGRS's infinite-slope form, unchanged from the previous version of this page. What changed is where ψ comes from: it used to be a number you assumed, and is now a number the rainfall produces.

Cohesion is lognormal, so it stays positive and skews right; friction angle and unit weight are normal, clipped to 1–50° and to a floor of 1 kN/m³. The hydraulic properties are deterministic, as they are in TRIGRS, where they are grid values rather than random variables — so ψ is one number per scenario and the Monte Carlo runs over strength alone. Because FS falls monotonically with ψ, each sample has a single pressure head at which it reaches FS = 1; those are sorted once, which is what makes every curve on this page exact rather than resampled.

The random number stream is seeded, so a given seed and sample count reproduce exactly. Material properties are drawn once and reused by all three scenarios, which is what makes the comparison a controlled one: the same ten thousand slopes, wetter or better drained.
